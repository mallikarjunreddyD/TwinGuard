# TwinGuard: technical evidence and physical-validation programme

## Project overview

TwinGuard is an integrated, uncertainty-aware industrial IoT digital twin for condition monitoring and inspection planning for boilers and pressure equipment. Its implemented research prototype combines equipment geometry, virtual LiDAR and thermal inspection, fixed process sensing, geometric and operating analytics, hazard-aware evidence fusion, a persistent local edge platform, an interactive dashboard, and risk-adaptive inspection recommendations. The demonstrated environment is synthetic software and laboratory integration. Measurements represent simulated physical processes; working communications, storage, services and browser interactions are actual software executions. No industrial failure or boiler-explosion prediction capability has been demonstrated.

## Implemented prototype

Development proceeded from reproducible plant geometry and fault scenarios to imperfect virtual sensors, uncertainty-qualified analytics, temporal evidence fusion, industrial-protocol integration, and inspection planning. Phase 7 added independent healthy calibration, held-out detector comparisons, paired planner studies, acquisition-level ablations, operational boundary tests, recovery verification and integrated demonstrations. The processing chain is sensor acquisition → ingestion → analytics → evidence fusion → condition assessment → inspection recommendation → visualization. Public observations and accepted engineering context feed inference; restricted simulator truth is reserved for evaluation after predictions have been sealed.

The edge platform implements authenticated MQTT ingestion and read-only OPC UA access, persistent observations, asynchronous processing, event history, artifact provenance and a browser-based digital twin. Laboratory encrypted communication was exercised, alongside access-control and malformed-input rejection. These are concrete integration capabilities, but neither synthetic sensor values nor laboratory certificates establish instrument accuracy or approved site commissioning. Inspection recommendations support review and simulated reinspection; autonomous industrial flight or physical plant intervention is outside the demonstrated scope.

## Quantitative experimental evidence

The independent Phase 7 geometric comparison contains 1,140 scan pairs, including 840 fault cases and 300 healthy cases. B3.1 localized faults in 635/840 cases, an unconditional recall of 75.6%, with positive findings on 27/300 healthy scans. Candidate B3.2 localized 235/840 faults, produced positives on 4/300 healthy scans, and returned inconclusive outcomes on 840/1,140 scans. Its reduced false positives therefore accompany substantial abstention and lost unconditional sensitivity. B3.2 was not promoted to the default detector. Within identical supported domains, healthy positives decreased from 18/60 to 4/60 while localized detections changed from 237/240 to 235/240. This narrower result supports continued calibration research, not general superiority. Earlier Phase 3.1 improvements used a different experimental distribution and cannot be substituted for these independent results.

Original E2 experiments evaluated operating-state conditioning on 30 held-out synthetic sequences and 150 thermal frames. In normal load-transition scenarios, static operating false-positive rate was 81.3%, while conditioning reduced it to 0%, with 1.0% abstention. Thermal transition false positives fell from 20/25 frames to 0/25, with 8% inconclusive. The tested hotspot condition yielded 15/15 positive thermal frames detected; conditioned mean mask intersection-over-union was 0.439. These results establish benefits only within the simulated regimes and calibration range. Degraded or out-of-range measurements can become inconclusive. Thermal image regions do not automatically provide verified physical association to equipment surfaces, and these acquisition-based results are not service-latency measurements.

The original upstream-analytics E3-B comparison used 330 positive principal-event scenarios. F0 recognized 32/330 and F1 recognized 270/330, with F1 severity-qualified precision of 83.08%. Matching required the appropriate hypothesis, graded asset or inspection scope, and event window; critical matches required red severity, and geometry required qualified overlap with the evaluator's true footprint. Unmatched warnings count against strict precision, including some real secondary findings that did not match the principal event. This distinction prevents unrelated warnings from concealing a missed principal hazard. It is synthetic policy and integration evidence, not a physical safety-reliability estimate.

The final lighter full edge test retained current status for 186/186 critical readings. A heavier mixed inspection workload retained only 145/184, leaving 39 stale despite unchanged freshness requirements. Queue/service pressure remains a measured limitation. Controlled restarts, duplicate handling, actual protocol exchanges, persistent event records and real browser rendering were exercised. Verified storage work includes bounded archival of 5,120 inbox records and SQL/artifact recovery checks; long-duration trace growth remains unresolved. These successes support a functioning research platform while identifying the capacity work required before representative edge deployment.

Earlier E5 planning increased mean qualified area from approximately 0.7344 m² per trial for P0 to 0.8208 m² for P3 without additional overall discovery. Phase 7 completed 240 main paired cases across five planners and two common detectors, plus supplemental controls, real acquisition ablations and all 30 fresh-chronology controls. P3v2 considers measurement support and cost, but its benefits are conditional and mixed. Under common B3.2, P3v2 localized faults in 23/120 cases using 148 views, compared with 31/120 using 360 views for P3 and 85/120 using 360 for P0. Fewer acquisitions and fewer healthy positives do not establish superior fault discovery. Qualified coverage, new discovery, confirmation of known evidence and cumulative detection are reported separately.

## Engineering contributions

The demonstrated contribution is an integrated research capability: reproducible geometry and virtual sensing, localized uncertainty-qualified detection, calibration and evidence-quality awareness, operating-state conditioning, hazard-specific temporal fusion, explainable advisories, durable local integration, and risk/coverage-aware inspection planning. Unsupported measurements remain explicit rather than being silently interpreted as healthy. Sealed predictions, independent evaluation truth, preserved failures, versioned configurations and artifact hashes make the findings auditable. Scientific novelty is not asserted for every implemented component; further research must establish which integration choices improve outcomes beyond appropriate baselines.

## Current limitations

Physical repeatability, deformation sensitivity and cross-modality calibration remain unmeasured. L2 geometric conditions frequently become inconclusive; current detection concerns localized relative surface change. Broad deformation, global motion and support displacement require distinct observability studies. Cross-view calibration is limited and support is unequal across assets. No universal planner fault-discovery advantage has been demonstrated. Operational gaps include heavy-load critical staleness, uncertain long-duration storage growth, and uncommissioned site identities, clocks, sensors and engineering limits. Synthetic scores cannot certify hardware accuracy or industrial safety. These observed limitations define the next experiments rather than serving as assumptions of success.

## Equipment-dependent validation

Equipment selection should follow a documented measurement uncertainty budget and approved scope. Procurement alone cannot establish sensitivity to 10–20 mm displacement. Precision reference metrology and an inspection payload serve different roles; UAV suitability does not imply adequate deformation accuracy.

| Category | Purpose and present gap | Physical experiment and expected evidence | Milestone; scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| LiDAR measurement system | Real repeatability, coverage and registration are unknown | Repeated controlled scans against independently surveyed displacement; error, abstention and detection curves | M1–M2; essential for geometry |
| Radiometric thermal imaging | Synthetic temperature and image quality lack physical calibration | Reference-temperature and surface experiments; radiometric error and spatial-association uncertainty | M1, M3; essential for thermal scope |
| Industrial IoT instrumentation | Process channels currently originate in simulation | Calibrated pressure, temperature, vibration and relevant operating measurements; quality and timing records | M3–M4; essential for process scope |
| Edge computing and communications | Workstation timing does not represent deployment | Intended compute/network and synchronized clocks; sustained-load freshness and recovery measurements | M5; essential |
| Reference targets and deformation apparatus | No independent physical displacement truth exists | Surveyed specimens at known 10, 20, 30 and 50 mm displacement; traceable metrology and uncertainty | M1–M2; essential |
| UAV or controlled positioning equipment | Physical viewpoint benefit and flight operation untested | Authorized repeated viewpoints, occlusion and reviewed reinspection; measured acquisition utility | M6; UAV optional, controlled positioning sufficient initially |

## Future validation milestones

M1 establishes instrument calibration, reference metrology, synchronization and uncertainty budgets with traceable records. M2 measures controlled geometric displacement across view, range, surface and environmental conditions, reporting misses, false positives and inconclusiveness. M3 integrates physical thermal and process measurements with calibration and spatial-association evidence. M4 evaluates multimodal evidence under controlled operating changes and fault conditions, with independent truth and strict event matching. M5 measures freshness, admission, throughput, recovery and storage growth on representative edge hardware and networks. M6, where authorized, compares physical viewpoints and reviewed reinspection using common budgets and detector settings. M7 demonstrates the integrated system in an appropriate relevant environment, with site-approved limits, security, procedures and acceptance criteria. Each milestone requires measured evidence and may reveal failure or restricted applicability; none is presumed successful.

## TRL positioning and technical justification

The integrated laboratory software evidence supports the conservative Phase 7 assessment consistent with TRL 4. Relevant-environment integration and performance verification are needed for TRL 5/6; physical TRL 6 remains a proposed objective. The work merits further research investment because an operating integrated demonstrator, reproducible synthetic evaluations and explicit failure mechanisms already exist. Funding would support the defined transition to traceable physical measurement, equipment-dependent validation and representative operational testing. The proposal is justified by that executable research foundation and measurable next steps, rather than by unsupported claims of industrial readiness.
